HOW IT WORKS

Four stages, one command

01 · Clean

02 · Align or quantify

03 · Count and test

04 · Look further

Raw reads are profiled, then trimmed of adapters and low-quality bases.

FastQC · fastp

One of four aligners, chosen by one flag. Strandedness is inferred per sample.

STAR · HISAT2 · Salmon · Kallisto

One gene count matrix, tested by two independent callers in parallel.

featureCounts · tximport · DESeq2 · edgeR

Splicing, transcript usage, fusions and pathways, then one report over it all.

rMATS · DEXSeq · fgsea · Quarto

Stop early with --stop_at preQC | postQC | DE | GSEA; start late with --counts.

rnaseq-flow · UCL ARC

Under the hood, four stages. FastQC profiles the raw reads and fastp trims them. Then one of four aligners: two genome aligners, STAR and HISAT2, or two pseudo-aligners, Salmon and Kallisto. On the genome routes RSeQC works out the strandedness of each sample and feeds it straight into featureCounts. Third, one gene count matrix, from featureCounts or from tximport, tested by DESeq2 with apeglm shrinkage and by edgeR's quasi-likelihood test, in parallel and independently, so genes both call are your most robust hits. Finally the analyses gene-level counts can't see: rMATS splicing events, edgeR diffSplice, DEXSeq transcript usage, isoform switching, STAR-Fusion, then GSEA and gProfiler, and one Quarto report over all of it. You can stop early for a quick QC pass, or start late from a count matrix.
