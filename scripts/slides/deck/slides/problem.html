Most RNA-seq runs stop at a count matrix

The data arrives as FASTQ. The question is biological.

A matrix and a QC report, with the biology still to do.

Expression, splicing and fusions as separate runs and samplesheets.

A stranded library read as unstranded: plausible, and wrong.

An analysis reaches a paper with nobody named for it.

rnaseq-flow · UCL ARC

Most RNA-seq processing ends at a count matrix and a QC report, and that is not the question anyone asked. The widely used community pipeline, nf-core/rnaseq, says so itself: it produces a matrix and QC and does not assign significance. Differential expression, splicing and fusions are each a separate pipeline, with their own samplesheet and their own references, and every boundary is a chance for the annotation, the baseline or the sample labels to disagree. Some mistakes don't fail at all. A stranded library processed as unstranded gives splicing values that look entirely plausible and are wrong; rnaseq-flow itself had exactly that bug in rMATS until 1.9.0. And the analyst, often core facility staff, does the work that ends up in a paper without an author line or any machine-readable record.
